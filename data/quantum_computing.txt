Quantum Computing Architecture and Error Mitigation
Quantum processors utilize superconducting transmon qubits cooled to 15 millikelvin using dilution refrigerators.
The primary challenge in quantum computing is quantum decoherence, caused by thermal fluctuations and electromagnetic interference.
Surface codes are the most widely studied quantum error correction architecture, requiring approximately 1,000 physical qubits to encode a single fault-tolerant logical qubit.
High-fidelity two-qubit gate operations achieve error rates below 0.5% using cross-resonance microwave pulses.
Cryogenic CMOS control circuits are placed adjacent to the quantum plane to reduce wire congestion and thermal load.
Quantum advantage benchmarks focus on random circuit sampling and molecular energy simulation for lithium hydride.
The quantum research team is led by Dr. Elena Rostova at the Advanced Cryogenics Facility in Zurich.
