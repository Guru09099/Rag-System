Modern Clean Energy Grid Storage and Distribution
The renewable microgrid combines utility-scale lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery storage with pumped hydroelectric reserves.
The primary grid battery installation has a capacity of 400 megawatt-hours with a continuous discharge rate of 100 megawatts.
Grid frequency stability is maintained within 49.8 Hz to 50.2 Hz using grid-forming inverters with synthetic inertia algorithms.
Solar photovoltaic arrays utilize bifacial monocrystalline silicon panels operating at an average efficiency of 22.4%.
Wind turbines on the northern ridge feature permanent-magnet synchronous generators producing power at wind speeds between 3 and 25 meters per second.
Black-start capability is guaranteed by an auxiliary 10-megawatt flywheel storage unit capable of responding within 20 milliseconds.
The chief grid engineer is Marcus Vance, stationed at the Pacific Regional Operations Center in Oregon.
